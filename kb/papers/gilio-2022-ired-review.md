Open-access perspective tracing IRED/RedAm reductive amination from discovery
to ton-scale industrial processes, including the role of structure and protein
engineering.

## Key claims

- Asymmetric reductive amination is a key route to chiral amines for pharma;
  IREDs and reductive aminases (RedAms) turned this into an industrial
  biocatalysis platform.
- Reviews the contribution of structure and protein engineering to activity,
  selectivity, and stability of process-suitable IRED catalysts.

## What it says about IRED-88 (Section on the Novartis ZPL389 case)

- IRED88 was found by library screening plus genome mining: 70% conversion to
  the desired (R)-product of ketone + methylamine (ZPL389, an H4 receptor
  antagonist for atopic dermatitis), but only 30% ee.
- The DMS (all positions x 19 alternatives, screened by mass spectrometry)
  revealed **S220T, at the mouth of the active site cleft**, lifting ee to
  96%; the improvement was attributed to restricted access for the pro-S
  substrate conformer.
- Three epPCR rounds on S220T gave **Q194L/S220T/H230Y**: 85% conversion,
  99% ee, 1 g scale, 72% yield, >99% ee final; Tm rose to 57 C vs 42 C for
  wild type, with two-fold higher specific activity.
- ML-guided variants alone gave lower ee; **ML coupled with
  structure-guided evolution** produced superior mutants such as
  **M129L/A156S/Y177W** (59% conversion, 93% ee).
- Conclusions: ML is useful as a sample-efficient variant generator; hits
  must be validated at scale; both activity and stability improvements
  matter; **linear additivity holds for loci outside the active site** but is
  harder to predict inside it, where cooperative stereoselectivity mechanisms
  operate.
